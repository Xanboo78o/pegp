ARTICLE 1

THE LEAGUE

The Pembroke Grand Prix is a push-kart racing championship. Teams of two build their own kart, bring it to the circuit, and race wheel-to-wheel for the Season One title.

The circuit is a street circuit: it appears on race morning — cones out, Director's box up — and is gone by dinner. The layout can change between rounds. Every circuit gets a name, and every circuit keeps its own lap record. The grass is borrowed; we leave it how we found it.

ARTICLE 2

THE FORMULA — kart rules

  • Human push power only. No motors, no batteries, no stored energy of any kind. The engine is a person.
  • One rider, riding. The kart carries the co-driver. If nobody's in it, it's not a kart, it's a cart.
  • The rider must be able to stop the kart. Feet, drag-stick, lever — the how is your team's engineering. But a kart with no brakes doesn't pass scrutineering.
  • Nothing sharp, nothing pointy. The Director inspects every kart at check-in and can refuse anything that looks like it wants to hurt somebody.
  • Riders wear helmets. Non-negotiable. No helmet, no grid. Every real league scrutineers; this is ours.

That is the entire formula. Wheels, materials, shape, aero, paint, tarp budget — all of it is your team's problem and your team's advantage. Build something cursed.

ARTICLE 3

THE CREW — driver & co-driver

  • The DRIVER pushes. They are the engine and the steering. They run the racing line.
  • The CO-DRIVER rides. They are the brakes and the eyes — they control corner entry and call the traffic the driver can't see.
  • A pit swap means the driver and co-driver trade places. Fresh legs are a strategy.
ARTICLE 4

THE RACE WEEKEND

QUALIFYING — each team runs the circuit alone against the clock. Best single lap counts and sets the grid. Cone hit: +2 seconds on that lap. All four wheels off track: that lap is deleted (track limits — say it in the FIA voice).

THE HEATS — everyone on track together. Grid start under the five lights; when the lights are out, it's racing. First across the line after the last lap wins. A race day can run as many heats as legs allow; tradition says heat two runs the reverse grid.

THE PEMBROKE 10 — the season finale. Ten laps, every team, minimum one pit swap, maximum three. Double points. The long one.

ARTICLE 6

PENALTIES

In the heats, penalties cost one position at the flag, applied by Race Control:

  • Ramming / deliberate contact. Karts bump — racing incident. Karts used as weapons — penalty.
  • Corner cut that gains a position.
  • Jump start — moving before the lights are out.
  • Abandoned kart — stopping and getting out mid-race, unless it's an emergency.
⚠ NEVER contact a pusher. A kart is a machine; a driver at full sprint is a person. Kart-on-pusher contact is an automatic DSQ, no discussion.

Emergencies are always free. If someone's hurt or about to be, stop — red flag, clock stops, no penalty ever. Safety outranks the championship.

ARTICLE 7

CONDUCT — rivalry & crime

  • Trash talk is encouraged. Rivalry is the sport. Team radio screaming is expected.
  • Personal insults are not rivalry. First offense: warning from the Director. Second: DSQ from the round.
  • Espionage is legal. Sabotage is fatal. Looking at another team's kart, photographing it, copying its ideas — that's racing, that's why tarps exist. Touching another team's kart: instant DSQ, and the Director decides when — or if — you come back.
🛠 Tarps are officially endorsed. Protect your secrets.
ARTICLE 8

RACE CONTROL

The console at the Director's box runs the day: it calls teams to the line, runs the clock and the start lights, records laps, issues penalties, and announces everything out loud. The Director's word is final. Complaints may be filed directly into the mulch.

ONE FIELD. TWO LEGS. NO MERCY.
