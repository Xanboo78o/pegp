THE MAP

TRACK MAP — same orientation as the satellite (road left · trees right · Eagles bottom)

Lap ≈ 370 ft — expect 33–42 s laps. Tight and technical on purpose: Monaco pace, not Monza pace. Legs survive. HEAT LAPS = 4 recommended. Max 3–4 karts per heat on a figure-8 — run split heats if the grid is bigger. The whole south third of the field stays unused: spectator lawn now, Circuit Two later.

THE CORNERS

CORNER GUIDE — one lap

  • S/F — Garden 1's east face, heading north. The Director's orange arrow, honored.
  • T1 · THE CROWN — left over the top of Garden 1, drifting downhill. The exit cones tighten ON PURPOSE: they force you slow before the wall section. Enter the Monaco hot and the wall will introduce itself.
  • T2 · THE MONACO — the narrow walled passage down Garden 1's road side. One kart wide. Box wall on your right, garden on your left, nothing else. OVERTAKING BANNED between the walls — win your place before it or after it, exactly like the real one.
  • T3 · GARDEN GATE — left around Garden 1's bottom corner and then the climb east along its south face. Uphill exit — carry everything, this drag decides whether anyone can attack you into the Plunge.
  • ✕ THE CROSSOVER — you cross your own track at right angles. Rules below. This is the signature of the whole circuit.
  • T4 · THE PLUNGE — sweeping right after the X, pouring south down the long east lane. Fastest stretch; the only real slipstream-pressure zone. Divebomb entry into the Horseshoe lives at the end of it.
  • T5 · THE HORSESHOE — 180° right at the bottom, box wall on the south side, then THE CLIMB: north past Garden 2's shoulder, uphill-biased, back toward the X and the flag. The lap's second leg-check.
THE X

CROSSOVER RULES — read twice

✕ THE CLIMBER HAS RIGHT OF WAY. Always. A kart climbing to the flag cannot afford to stop; a kart exiting the Gate can re-accelerate for free. Downhill yields. No exceptions, no "I was there first."
  • The call: every rider approaching the X shouts "CROSSING!" — both lanes, every lap, even alone. It's the habit that prevents the one bad day.
  • No crossing guard needed — the geometry IS the marshal. The lap spreads karts out: nobody reaches the end of the Climb while anyone's still in the 1-2-3 section. Per the Director, if you ARE still in the small loop when the field hits the X, it's because A) your driver is 5, B) your rider is holding down the brakes, or C) you've got a skill issue.
  • Which means meeting traffic at the X = you're being LAPPED — and the blue flag rule from the Racing School already covers it: the lapped kart yields to the leaders. The X is a blue-flag zone by definition.
  • Contact at the X = automatic penalty on whoever had the yield, and the Director's mood decides how bad. Don't be the reason the figure-8 gets deleted.

⚠ HAZARD PROTOCOL — verify the pole-shaped thing at the paddock's north edge (marked ⚠). If real: noodle-wrap it; it sits OFF both lanes but next to where people stand. Walk the top pocket + Monaco stretch for guy-wire anchors before first practice. A cable is invisible at sprint speed.
🛡 THE BUFFER LAW — six paces off every curb stays sacred everywhere except the walled Monaco, which buys its exception with a continuous wall, single-file, and a ban on overtaking. Downhill never points at a road. The south field is out of bounds — it's where spectators and Garden 3 live.
