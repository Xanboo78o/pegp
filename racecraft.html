LESSON 1

READING THE TRACK

THE RACING LINE The fastest path through a corner: enter wide, cut to the inside at the middle, exit wide again. Out-in-out. It turns a sharp corner into a gentler curve, and gentler means you keep more speed. On grass this is everything — speed you scrub off is agony to push back.
WHEN: every corner, every lap, forever. It's not a trick, it's the job.
APEX The exact point where your line touches the inside of the corner. Hit it and the corner is one smooth arc. Miss it early and you run out of room on exit; miss it late and you parked in the middle for nothing.
LATE APEX Aiming for an apex deeper into the corner than normal: slower in, but pointed straighter at the exit, so you launch out harder.
WHEN: any corner that leads onto the uphill. Exit speed onto a climb is worth double — carry it or die on the slope. Also the best defensive line: a late apex leaves no door open behind you.
CORNER TYPES Hairpin — near-U-turn (a mulch bed makes a perfect one). Slow, huge overtaking spot. Chicane — quick left-right flick. Rhythm section; cutting it is how corner-cut penalties get born. Sweeper — long fast curve, held at speed. Esses — linked S-curves where the exit of one IS the entry of the next; mess up the first and you pay through all of them.
LESSON 2

GOING FAST ALONE — qualifying craft

Slow in, fast out. The amateur brakes late and exits slow. The champion brakes early-ish and exits like a slingshot. Straights are decided in the corner before them.

Momentum is money. Grass taxes acceleration hard. A kart that flows at 90% pace everywhere beats a kart that sprints and stalls. Float like a Cadillac. Sting like a Beemer.

The engine is cardio. A quali run is an interval sprint — the pusher who burns everything on lap 1 sets their worst time on lap 3. Best lap usually comes on the lap where the driver ran smart, not hard.

Track evolution is real: each run flattens the grass on the line and the track gets faster all day. Late runs are quick runs. Directors know this. Now you do.

LESSON 3

WHEEL-TO-WHEEL COMBAT

OUTBRAKING Passing by braking later than the kart ahead into a corner. You draw alongside in the braking zone and own the inside by the apex. This is the bread-and-butter overtake — and it's a CO-DRIVER skill, because the rider is the brakes. Late brakers win places; too-late brakers meet mulch.
WHEN: end of the downhill run, where speeds are highest and braking zones are longest. Biggest speed difference = biggest opportunity.
DIVEBOMB An outbrake turned up to 11: a late, deep lunge from way back down the inside, arriving at the apex at the same time as the defender — who didn't know you were coming. High risk, high glory, extremely funny when it works.
WHEN: last lap, hairpin, nothing to lose.
THE LINE: you must arrive IN CONTROL and make the corner. Arriving as an unguided missile that torpedoes the other kart = contact penalty. A divebomb is a scalpel, not a cannonball.
THE SWITCHBACK (CUTBACK) The counter to a defender hugging the inside: let them have the tight line — it ruins their exit — while you sweep wide, carry momentum, and drive past them on the way out.
WHEN: someone defends the inside of a hairpin. On grass this move is KING, because their slow tight exit costs them double. Defending too hard loses races here.
DEFENDING & THE ONE-MOVE RULE The defender may pick their line — including parking on the inside so there's no door. What they may NOT do is weave. One move to defend, then hold it. Swerving back and forth in front of a sprinting pusher is how ankles die.
Weaving = the Director's contact-penalty finger gets twitchy.
THE TOW / PRESSURE At push-kart speed there's no real slipstream — but following one kart-length back is still a weapon. You run their pace for free, you see their mistakes first, and the rider ahead has to keep looking backwards. Pressure causes mistakes. Sit. Wait. Pounce.
TURN ONE, LAP ONE Every kart arrives at the first corner at once. You cannot win the race here. You can absolutely lose it here.
Grid P1-P2: take your line, go. Midfield: survive, exit clean, and let the heroes crash into each other in front of you.
LESSON 4

THE CREW GAME — driver & co-driver

The pusher is head-down at full sprint. They see grass. The rider sees the RACE. A silent kart is a slow kart — the fast crews sound like this:

  • "BRAKE... NOW" — the rider calls braking; they own it, they time it.
  • "INSIDE!" / "OUTSIDE!" — a kart is attacking, and which door they picked.
  • "CLEAR" — attack over, take the normal line back.
  • "YELLOW" / "RED" — rider watches the marshal's flags. The pusher never has to look.
  • "SEND IT" — the official signal to attempt something stupid.

Pit swap execution (Pembroke 10): swaps only in the pit box, and a swap costs whatever your crew's changeover time is — so PRACTICE it. Rider out, pusher in, ten seconds or four. That difference is free lap time, and it's the one part of racing you can train in a driveway.

LESSON 5

THE LONG GAME — strategy words

STINT The stretch one pusher runs between swaps. The whole Pembroke 10 is stint math: 10 laps, up to 3 swaps, two sets of legs.
THE UNDERCUT In F1: pit BEFORE your rival so your fresh tires gain time while they stay out on dead ones. In PeGP: swap before your pusher blows up. Fresh legs immediately punch faster laps while the rival's tired engine wheezes on. By the time THEY swap, you're gone.
WHEN: your pusher's laps start dropping off. Swap one lap BEFORE the wall, not one after. The rider can hear the breathing — that's data.
THE OVERCUT The opposite bet: STAY OUT longer because your engine is a cardio monster who holds pace. While rivals lose time swapping, you bank clean laps.
WHEN: your pusher runs cross-country and you know it. This is the flex play.
TIRE MANAGEMENT In F1 you save the rubber. In PeGP the tires are a PERSON. Pace the sprint: push hard out of slow corners (where it pays most), breathe on the straights, never redline for a lap that doesn't matter. The team whose lap 9 looks like their lap 2 wins the finale.
TRACK POSITION vs PACE The eternal question: is it better to be FAST or to be IN FRONT? In front means clean air and control; fast means nothing if you're stuck behind a rolling roadblock. Quali hard, start well, and you get both.
LESSON 6

KNOW YOUR FLAGS

Flags are waved by the marshal (whoever's not racing this heat — it's a real job, take it seriously). The RIDER watches flags and calls them. Ignoring a flag is ignoring the Director.

GREEN — racing. The world is as it should be.
YELLOW — danger on track (spun kart, dropped human). No overtaking, hold position, slow down until you're past the mess.
RED — session STOPPED. Everyone halts where safe. Clock stops. Usually means someone needs a hand — flags outrank the championship.
WHITE — final lap. Send everything you've got left.
BLUE — you're being LAPPED. The leaders are coming through; don't fight karts that are a lap ahead of you.
CHECKERED — it's over. Someone's a legend. Cool-down lap, shake hands, argue in the paddock.
🚩 FLAG KIT: garden stakes or broom handles + cloth squares (old shirts, felt, a sacrificed pillowcase). Checkered = white cloth, tape grid, black paint. Total cost: basically nothing. Marshal duty rotates.
APPENDIX

THE DICTIONARY — sound like you belong

  • GRID — the starting lineup, set by qualifying.
  • POLE — P1 on the grid. Fastest qualifier. Worth +3 points and eternal smugness.
  • BOX — the pit box. "BOX BOX BOX" = come in and swap, screamed rider-to-pusher.
  • DNF — Did Not Finish. The kart gave up, or the engine did.
  • DSQ — disqualified. You know what you did.
  • SCRUTINEERING — the Director's kart inspection at check-in. Pass it or park it.
  • PARC FERMÉ — karts under tarps, secrets protected, hands OFF (Article 7).
  • UNDERSTEER — the kart refuses to turn and plows straight on (grass loves this — slow down, then steer).
  • OVERSTEER — the back steps out and tries to overtake the front. Fun. Occasionally on purpose.
  • RACING INCIDENT — contact that was nobody's fault. The Director's favorite verdict.
  • FLAT SPOT — what your legs have on Monday.
  • THE WALL — what a pusher hits around lap 7. Not made of bricks. Worse.
